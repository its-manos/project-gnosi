## Source criticism and interpretive boundaries
- Distinguish Aigai royal burial contexts and later restored palace from the early fourth-century material landscape. Pella's extant streets often show later phases.
- 'Macedonian' / 'Greek' ancient ethnic and political descriptions must not be projected onto modern national identities.
- Older writers (Diodorus, Justin, Plutarch) narrate through later political and biographical frameworks; Demosthenes and Aeschines argue their own responsibility and reputation.
- Philip's military and fiscal system developed over time. Neither 'invented the sarissa in one winter' nor 'a single battle unified Greece' follows from the evidence.
- The north had plural cities and leagues with independent political agency. Olynthus' destruction meant severe losses for its residents.
- Phocian, Thessalian, Theban and Athenian interests in the Third Sacred War differed. The Peace of Philocrates did not end dissent.
- The 338 outcome is secure; proposed precise battlefield maneuvers are reconstructions.
- The League's common-peace oath partially survives as an inscription (IG II² 236); its legal language must be read beside asymmetrical Macedonian enforcement. Sparta's distinct position rules out 'all Greeks'.
- Succession at 336 was uncertain; no Alexander campaigns or future kingdoms covered.
