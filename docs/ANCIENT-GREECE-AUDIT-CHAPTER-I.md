## Specific findings and recommended actions

### C1-01 — Question calibration on **actual** card types (medium priority)

- **#1 multiple-choice** on the Agora's functions uses three single categories and one obvious combination. This is a comprehension check, not necessarily truly `medium` in a Greek adult pilot. Review alternatives without turning the correct answer ambiguous.
- **#2 question 2**, tagged `hard`, asks why Athenians invoked the `Ἑλληνικόν` in Herodotus 8.144. The correct option is the sole concrete diplomatic purpose; all alternatives assert anachronistic unification, exact borders or no Greek civil conflict. The idea is useful but the distractors are easy to eliminate. Candidate for plausible alternate rhetorical motives **if each is demonstrably false in the passage**.
- **#3 questions 1 and 3** use actual `ordering` and `matching` `presentation` objects. The legacy four-option summaries are not adequate difficulty evidence. Do not revise format or fingerprints on that basis; assess user-facing item ordering and matching on device.
- **#4 question 1**, `spot-the-mistake` tagged `hard`, contrasts a sweeping “all Cretan centres simultaneously” claim against three cautious true statements. Consider a less cartoonish false inference that still has one unambiguously incorrect sentence.
- **#5 question 4**, `multiple-choice` tagged `hard`, puts the nuanced correct response beside three absolute propositions (“never affected,” “all direct,” “every part equally safe”). The item likely tests recognition of absolutes more than route interpretation. Ask the learner to judge **competing plausible landing approaches** in a specified coastal scenario. **#5 question 3 is genuine typed matching** and cannot be called trivial from its numeric fallback alone.

**Do not rewrite quiz objects or correct indices in this audit pass.** If a later edit changes scored wording/meaning, preserve frozen old results and use explicit version/fingerprint compatibility tests.

### C1-02 — Cross-lesson epistemic refrain (medium priority; semantic overlap, not copying)

Several paragraphs say in different words that an isolated object, language, map or image does not prove every detail about the past. That is an intentional recurring educational principle, **not** a confirmed redundant passage. However, its sixth repetition may begin to feel like a disclaimer rather than a new insight.

A differentiation rule for later copyediting:
- #1: **stratigraphy and changing uses** of House Θ / household social limits.
- #2: **identity under political pressure** (Herodotus' diplomatic text is situated testimony, not objective national-census data).
- #3: **the method itself** (ostraka handwriting, speeches, epigraphy and comparative evidence).
- #4: **periodization** and the difference between missing archives and missing people.
- #5: **historical geography** and the inferential difference between viable routes and documented voyages.

Keep the method reinforced through the *new* task in each lesson. Remove only filler statements that repeat the preceding conclusion without extending it, especially around #5's “straight line is not an actual route” point.

### C1-05 — Primary evidence spot-checks (passed **only for these bounded claims**)

1. **#1 House Θ:** the Acropolis Museum's [actual object/site record](https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/en/buildings/house-th) supports foundation in the late fifth century BCE, centuries of later modifications, the andron and the third-century BCE workshop basins with *possible* cloth washing. The lesson correctly says the installation is evidence and the fullonica use an interpretation. This is **one checked claim cluster**, not a certificate for the entire lesson.
2. **#2 identity and alliance:** [Herodotus, Histories 8.144, original Greek](https://folio2.furman.edu/dramaturg/Herodotus/pages/chunk_1007.html) is accessible and presents the `Ἑλληνικόν` in an exchange with Sparta over Persian diplomacy. The lesson correctly treats this as a situated historical speech, not a legal charter for a Greek nation-state.
3. **#3 Themistocles ostraka:** the [American School Lang 1990 catalogue](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/publications/book/?i=9780876612255) describes **191** ostraka, mostly for Themistocles, found on the Acropolis north slope; “περίπου 190” is a legitimate rounding. The [Bryn Mawr Classical Review of Missiou](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2011/2011.11.02/) explicitly notes the contested earlier attribution to 14 hands; the text's uncertainty is warranted. The [original Thucydides 1.22](https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0003.tlg001.perseus-grc2/chapter:1.22/) supports the distinction between speech reconstruction and verbatim record.
4. **#4 chronology:** the [Met chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/04/eusb.html) gives Geometric c. 900–700, Archaic c. 700–480, Classical c. 480–323 BCE and Hellenistic from 323 BCE, consistent with the lesson's **conventional** boundaries. [Waal's study of writing discontinuity](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198908746.003.0005) is available in substantial public text and emphasises debate about *date of adoption* of alphabetic writing. The lesson appropriately describes the first **surviving securely dated** inscriptions rather than claiming certain date of invention.
5. **#5 west Samos:** the peer-reviewed [Michael Loy 2024 article](https://doi.org/10.1080/00253359.2024.2331910) is accessible in HTML, with a [Cambridge accepted-version repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/a77d566a-8db2-4ac8-9575-c63b3ee2a79d). It supports dangerous west-coast wind/anchorage constraints, routes inferred using archaeology/environment/early-modern comparisons, and evidence of maritime connections. The lesson correctly avoids claiming to know a particular ship's exact itinerary.

**Research access rule:** above refers only to pages actually inspected (the external article itself, museum page, primary passage, academic reviews or a listed catalogue). It does **not** claim full-text reading of every paper in the five lessons' bibliographies. The 17 answers have not undergone external question-by-question expert sign-off.
