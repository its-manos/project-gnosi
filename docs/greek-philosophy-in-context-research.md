## Learning objective and editorial rationale

Understand Greek philosophy as **social practices**—travelling instruction, questioning and refutation, textual circulation, intellectual association, observation and disagreement—across multiple poleis and centuries. Distinguish literary presentations of speech from verbatim records, compare Academy and Lyceum with their actual architectural and institutional contexts, and evaluate social exclusion and selective textual survival. Neither a philosopher-of-the-week catalogue nor a précis of metaphysical doctrines.

**Final title:** «Πού γινόταν η φιλοσοφία;» (working title «Φιλοσοφία στους δρόμους και στις σχολές»). It invites a concrete spatial question and avoids implying that the entire philosophical tradition occurred in today's educational institutions.

**Narrative (six shifts, 30 paragraphs):**
1. `phil-01–05`: Literary scene of Protagoras in Callias' house; professional/itinerant sophists; unequal instruction; critique is not neutral reporting.
2. `phil-06–10`: Miletus, Elea, poem and prose; the misleading “Presocratic” umbrella; natural/religious interpretation; the Derveni papyrus as an actual text-object with two dates.
3. `phil-11–15`: Socrates' historical existence, disputed portraits, questions rather than a catechism, the Socratic problem, tension and trial.
4. `phil-16–20`: Academy: public gymnasium and associated community, nearby private property, dialogue and writing, internal criticism, ruins vs speculative classroom.
5. `phil-21–25`: Lyceum: pre-existing sanctuary/gymnasium and 335 BCE community, mobility/Lesbos/Theophrastus, empirical inquiries and interpretative failures, manuscript complexity, changing palaestra.
6. `phil-26–30`: Gender, status, foreigners and enslaved populations; Aspasia and the **literary** Diotima; fragments/doxography and later manuscript transmission; reflect on who is absent.

**Reading-time estimate:** 1,520 whitespace-separated words across intro + 30 paragraphs; at 150–175 Greek words/minute = ~8.7–10.1 minutes, plus roughly 1–2 minutes for seven checks; coded `readingMinutes: 11`. Does not include extended pauses or zoom.

### Specific cautions kept visible

1. Socrates' exact views cannot be extracted from a single Platonic speech as verbatim fact. The “Socratic problem” remains active.
2. No monolithic “Greek science defeated religion” narrative: Derveni explicitly integrates exegesis, ritual discourse and cosmological language.
3. Plato's school was associated with the Academy's public place; Aristotle's group used an **existing** Lyceum. Modern institutional analogy is explicitly rejected.
4. Female philosophical participation is variously attested across **different centuries**, while Diotima as narrated by Plato has no secure independent historicity. No Hellenistic claims projected back to the Classical period.
5. The specific roles of slave labour in intellectual settings are poorly documented; the article avoids individual unsupported portraits and universal exclusion statements.
6. The date of the Derveni physical roll and the date/composition of its underlying commentary are different scholarly questions.
7. The preserved Aristotelian corpus is a later edited/copy-transmitted selection; it cannot simply be equated with classroom transcripts.
