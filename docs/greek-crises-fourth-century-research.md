## Identity and rationale
- Stable ID: `greek-crises-fourth-century` · Ιστορία → Αρχαία Ελλάδα · Chapter IV · 22/30.
- Working title: «Μετά την αθηναϊκή ήττα: μια ασταθής ισορροπία».
- Final title: «Μετά το 404: συμμαχίες, επεμβάσεις και εύθραυστες ειρήνες». Names the changing coalitions and enforced peaces without implying a simple succession of three empires.
- Objective: distinguish the 404 military settlement from the 403 constitutional reconciliation; explain the ambitions and limitations of Spartan, Persian and Theban power from the Corinthian War to Mantinea without a deterministic Macedonian ending.
- Structure: seven thematic sections; 34 stable paragraphs; 1916 Greek prose words; estimated 11 min at ~185 words/min excluding quiz. Seven reasoning checkpoints at 4, 9, 15, 20, 25, 30, 34.
- Narrative: surrender → oligarchic violence → contested reconciliation → regional/Persian agency → changing wartime coalitions → negotiated 'autonomy' → Thebes/Messene → 362 uncertainty. No retelling of #21 battles or preview of #23 Philip II.

## Claim-to-source map and disputed details
| Claim | Direct or scholarly support | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surrender conditions, the Thirty, armed democratic return (404–403) | Xenophon, Hellenica 2.2–2.4; Joyce 2022 pp. 38–67 | Xenophon has selective loyalties; avoid treating Spartan influence as the only causal force. |
| Lysias's brother Polemarchus, metic confiscations | Lysias 12, Against Eratosthenes | Adversarial prosecution, not disinterested statistical survey; individual story corroborates neither universal policy nor complete totals. |
| Reconciliation and `mē mnēsikakein` (403) | Athenaion Politeia 38–40; Stephen Todd 2015 OCD; Joyce 2022 | Retrospective constitutional narrative; specified exceptions and accountability, not universal forgiveness or equal citizenship. |
| Cyrus the Younger / Greek mercenaries (401) | Xenophon Anabasis 1–7; Hellenica 3 | First-person Greek account and narrow slice of Persian state; avoid simple Greek–Persian antagonism. |
| Agesilaus in Asia Minor, allied disagreements | Xenophon Hellenica 3–4; Buckler & Beck, Central Greece (2008) | Persian funding and local aims were variable. |
| Corinthian War, Cnidus (394) and Conon/Pharnabazus | Xenophon Hellenica 4; Hornblower OCD 2015 | Sea battle did not automatically restore fifth-century imperial institutions. |
| King's Peace 387/386 and autonomia | Xenophon Hellenica 5.1.31–36; Hornblower 2015; Buckler & Beck 2008 | Distinguish King's claims to Asian Greek cities from formal autonomy of other poleis; exceptions for Athens. |
| Cadmea 382 and liberation 379/378 | Xenophon Hellenica 5.2, 5.4; Buckler & Beck 2008 | Local oligarchic initiative and Theban coalition agency; source chronologies differ. |
| Athens' Second League invitation decree 378/7 | Attic Inscriptions Online RO 22 (= IG II² 43), Lambert & Rhodes | Restored/erased letters and political promise; not proof of uniformly noncoercive future enforcement. |
| Leuctra 371, Messene 369 | Xenophon Hellenica 6–7; Buckler & Beck 2008 | Tactical reconstruction contested; institutional changes not instantaneous for all Messenian labor relationships. |
| Mantinea 362, no durable pan-Greek settlement | Xenophon Hellenica 7.5.26–27; Huitink 2024 | Xenophon’s powerful literary ending is not a neutral universal verdict or prophecy of Macedonia. |
