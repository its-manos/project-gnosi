## Learning objective and editorial reasoning
Establish a geographical mental model: land and sea travel combine, accessible routes depend on vessel, weather, harbours, pass and direction, while historical networks are shaped by human choices. The original title «Η θάλασσα που ένωνε και χώριζε τις κοινότητες» was replaced with specific, concrete geographical obstacles and enablers. **Structure:** coast and inland routes → archipelago, seamanship and seasons → map-distance fallacy and Samos example → changing, multi-community networks. Distinct from preceding lesson's chronological survey. 18 stable paragraphs in four sections, four reasoning checkpoints (after paragraphs 4, 9, 13, 17). Greek text ~1 whitespace-separated words; reading estimate **7 minutes** (approximate, including checkpoint pauses).

**Methodological caution:** Papageorgiou models prehistoric possibilities, Loy examines archaic west Samos, and de Souza offers broader ancient Mediterranean navigation. They refer to different chronologies. Do not combine them into a single timeless route map; don't state universal winter closure or invented speeds/durations. Galaty explicitly cautions against geographic and political-economic homogenization. These are secondary academic sources; full closed-access articles may not all be readable, so claims stick to accessible summaries and passages.
