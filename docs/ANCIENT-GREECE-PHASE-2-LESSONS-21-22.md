## Lesson 21 — `peloponnesian-war`

Existing title retained: «Πελοποννησιακός Πόλεμος: φόβος, επιλογές και εξάντληση». The **35 stable paragraph IDs**, 7 checkpoints after paragraphs **4/9/14/19/24/29/35**, source records, image credits, two inline media anchors, chapter order, original seven question IDs and history are untouched.

The revised opening poses the central historical problem: why a war described as 431–404 BCE continued during the Peace of Nicias (421) and drew in independent regional decision-makers. Selective prose edits to `war-02` (Thucydides' 1.23 causal account vs inevitability), `war-08` (unresolved diagnosis of 430 epidemic), `war-17` (Melian Dialogue as organized historical argument, not stenography), `war-24` (Thucydides' literary arrangement of the Syracusan catastrophe), `war-31` (transition to Xenophon's source with its own selection) and `war-35` (stop before Lesson 22). This is neither a triumphalist retelling nor a claim that all citizens/captives had the same experience.

Sources and scholarly access:
- [Thucydides, *History*, 1.22–1.23; 2.47–53; 5.84–116; 6–8](https://adfonteslibrary.org/work-thucydides-history), accessible translated primary source, for reconstructed speeches, the epidemic and Melos, alliances and Sicilian War.
- [Oxford Classical Dictionary, “Peloponnesian War, 431–404 BCE,” Hornblower (2016), public extract](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/549687938): modern periodisation of Archidamian conflict, Nicias peace and the Sicilian/Decelean-Ionian phases. The full restricted text was **not** read.
- [Oxford Academic, *Democratic Collapse and Recovery in Ancient Athens (413–403)*, available extract](https://academic.oup.com/book/56192/chapter/443479365): 411 and 404 oligarchies must be distinguished; political actors exploited defeat, not an automatic constitutional conversion. Full restricted monograph not represented as read.
- [Livius, Thucydides on Sicily](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/thucydides-historian/the-colonization-of-sicily/): local Sicilian settlers and political agency; Syracuse cannot be reduced to a passive proxy of Sparta.
- [Yale Open Courses, Donald Kagan, causes study material](https://oyc.yale.edu/sites/default/files/10peloponnesianwari_1_0.pdf), source framing of competing origins. Reader text already cites primary records and contemporary Athenian casualty inscription.
- [Xenophon, *Hellenica*](https://adfonteslibrary.org/work-xenophon-hellenica) for the Athenian surrender after Thucydides' narrative stops at 411; genre and authorial bias remain separate.

## Lesson 22 — `greek-crises-fourth-century`

Existing title retained: «Μετά το 404: συμμαχίες, επεμβάσεις και εύθραυστες ειρήνες». **34 stable paragraph IDs**, 7 anchors **4/9/15/20/25/30/34**, sources and credits, inline photos Cadmea/Arcadian Gate, path position and old seven question IDs preserved. Intro now contrasts 404 defeat, 403 democracy restoration and autonomy promises of 378/7 in the Second Athenian League.

Targeted edits: `crisis-08` clarifies exceptions to the 403 amnesty and separates law from memory; `crisis-20` identifies the King's Peace's explicit Athenian island exceptions (Lemnos, Imbros, Skyros); `crisis-23` accurately specifies promised no tribute, garrison or governor in AIO RO 22, without asserting later total compliance; `crisis-27` signals debated tactical reconstruction of Leuctra; `crisis-32` distinguishes Xenophon's rhetorical concluding uncertainty at Mantinea 362 from a universal unchanged political status.

Research access:
- [Xenophon, *Hellenica*, Books II–VII](https://adfonteslibrary.org/work-xenophon-hellenica), primary later narrative of 403, Persian contacts, 386, Leuctra and Mantinea.
- [Attic Inscriptions Online, RO 22 / IG II² 43](https://www.atticinscriptions.com/inscription/RO/22), original Greek decree in translation and editorial notes, dated 378/7; the actual text prohibits Athenian garrisons/governors, tribute and some landholdings. Editors explicitly distinguish the **original public promise from later breaches**; the decree is not a receipt proving future behavior.
- [Oxford Classical Dictionary, Second Athenian Confederacy, Tuplin (2016), public extract](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/550498220), discusses alliance birth and later deviations; full restricted article not read.
- [Oxford Classical Dictionary, Leuctra, Lazenby (2016), abstract](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/549654654) dates the battle to **371 BCE** and discusses its consequences; [Banchich/Wiley summary](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781444338232.wbeow360) places confederate autonomy controversy in context, not a certain single cause.
- [Buckler & Beck, *Central Greece and the Politics of Power*, Cambridge excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/37057/excerpt/9780521837057_excerpt.pdf) for Boeotian alliances and local agency. Original bibliography/source links preserved. No access claims to paywalled full volumes.
- Greek historical acts in the 403 amnesty are not proof of universal forgiveness or equal civic status. The changed political position of independent Messene in 369 is not presumed instantly to erase every earlier labor dependence.

Quiz v2, same seven question IDs and same original numeric fallback positions `[1,2,3,0,1,2,3]`:
1. Spot: Spartan involvement in 404–403 did not eliminate local oligarchs' responsibility.
2. Fill: 403 `mē mnēsikakein` constrained prosecutions with exceptions.
3. Matching: Cyrus 401, Xenophon, satraps and Agesilaus — Greek/Persian entanglement.
4. Ordering: 401 → 395 → 394 (Cnidus) → 387/386 King's Peace.
5. Matching: King's Peace, Cadmea 382, RO 22 pledge and comparing practice.
6. Spot: 369 political independence of Messene did not uniformly transform every social relation at once.
7. Standard choice: Xenophon's 362 Mantinea ending isn't a prophecy or permanent constitutional verdict.
