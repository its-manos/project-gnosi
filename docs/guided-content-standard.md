# Guided lesson editorial standard

Architecture established this format; astronomy applies it to a second subject. Use it for future paths while adapting the structure to the discipline.

1. Start with an accessible introduction that explains what the field studies, why it matters and how its practitioners reason. Plan the whole sequence before drafting individual articles.
2. Progress from foundations toward deeper questions. Draw inspiration from credible curricula, adapt prerequisites for general readers, and write original examples rather than copying a course.
3. Write directly in natural Greek. Open with a concrete scene, question or puzzle. Define technical terms when they first become necessary. Use familiar comparisons with their limits made clear.
4. Let difficulty and information determine length. Use connected paragraphs, specific examples and purposeful sections; avoid padding, repetitive summaries and a uniform word quota. Reading estimates exclude quiz time.
5. Teach what each checkpoint tests before showing it. The current reader places one checkpoint after each pair of body paragraphs, so every new question needs its own taught pair. Use four to six meaningful questions when appropriate to this reader format.
6. Test interpretation, prediction, comparison and application. Wrong answers should reflect plausible misconceptions. Balance answer length and correct positions. Explain the reasoning, including assumptions and limits, rather than merely repeating the correct option.
7. Finish a substantial path with a case that connects earlier skills. Clearly label invented data or scenarios. Observation exercises are optional and never prerequisites for completion.
8. Choose images that teach or establish a useful context. Preserve full diagrams inline, distinguish photographs from reconstructions and processed maps, verify rights and credit creators. Bundle current assets for offline use.
9. Use primary sources for factual claims and link them in each lesson. Separate evidence, inference and uncertainty; avoid overstating a discovery or presenting an analogy as proof.
10. Preserve published IDs and the meaning/order of answer slots. A prose refresh must retain existing quizzes unless a deliberate, separately tested quiz-version change is required. Check existing results, partial attempts, unlocks, mistake practice and weekly review after any catalogue change.

Automated checks establish structural and progress compatibility. Editorial review and an on-device read remain necessary for language, explanation quality and mobile presentation.
