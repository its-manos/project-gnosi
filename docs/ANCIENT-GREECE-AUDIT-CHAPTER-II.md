## Registered findings

### C2-01 — Chronology/collapse/Geometric duplication risk (medium-high)

**Specific chain:** #4 `greek-chronology` already explains c. 1200 BCE loss of administrative archives and the later surviving alphabetic texts; #10 `palace-collapse` returns to the end of palatial institutions and surviving communities; #11 `geometric-greece` begins with continuing life without central palace archives and ends with the reappearance of alphabetic writing.

This is **not** literal duplication: #4 gives the period map; #10 examines specific destruction contexts and causal testing; #11 uses Lefkandi material and postpalatial writing. Preserve those distinct contributions. In a subsequent edit, consider shortening **repeated exposition** in #10's concluding recap and #11's opening while adding one explicit prior-lesson callback. Avoid rewriting #4 chronology purely to reduce overlap; it is a prerequisite.

### C2-02 — Question-label calibration, especially implausible distractors (medium)

Examples from current *numeric fallback* or normal-choice prompts:
- #6 `bronze-exchange-proof`: an obsidian source and find spot can indicate movement, but the three wrong responses insist upon a **single transporter**, **common monarch** or **only direct route**. At `hard`, this is likely too easy a discrimination for an adult Greek reader.
- #8 `mycenae-offices`: asserting `wanax` was certainly Homer's **Nestor** is conspicuously false relative to other cautious statements. The `spot-the-mistake` card may measure confidence in an extreme assertion more than institutional interpretation.
- #9 `linear-b-limits` tagged `hard` contrasts reasonable limitations with absolute claims about deciphering everything or Greek alphabet directly succeeding Linear B. Evaluate whether it genuinely requires reasoning or merely elimination.
- #10 `palace-pylos-archive` presents “Homer copied the archive” among obvious false choices. As **medium**, it could serve as an accessible reinforcement, but this distractor does not test the archaeological mechanism.
- #11 `geo-trade-inference` again makes direct personal travel an `unavoidably` true conclusion. If learners have just read #6/#5, the answer may be automatic despite `hard` label.

**Crucial distinction:** #6/#8/#9/#10/#11 also have native `matching` and `ordering` questions. Their four-option numeric fallback is **not** the actual native difficulty. ### C2-04 — Textbook label is not ancient identity or uniform state (low/medium)

#6 correctly distinguishes archaeological labels from historical polities. #7 correctly warns that a “Minoan” classification, modern Knossos reconstructions and present-day World Heritage framing do not demonstrate central rule over every Cretan region. The [UNESCO six-component Minoan Palatial Centres site](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1733/) explicitly was inscribed **2025** and confirms the key components, but it is a *heritage classification* and cannot alone prove a single unified Bronze Age state. Good continuity, avoid a second prolonged definition of the same caveat.

### C2-05 — Climate as context, not direct cause (historical spot-check passed in scope)

#10 correctly treats stalagmite evidence as evidence of changing regional environmental conditions, **not** proof that a specific drought triggered the Pylos fire or identifies its destroyer. The open full [Finné et al., PLOS ONE 2017 paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0189447) explicitly reports **little or no unequivocal synchronism** between dryness and the final palace destruction and discusses later aridity as a potential obstacle to renewed centralization. Preserve this nuance in any editorial compression.

### C2-06 — Linear B contribution sequence and independent verification (spot-check passed in scope)

The [Cambridge Classics decipherment introduction](https://www.classics.cam.ac.uk/research/projects/mycep/decipherment) supports Ventris' **1952** breakthrough, subsequent collaboration with Chadwick and publication in **1953**; [Cambridge's public history](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/cracking-the-code-the-decipherment-of-linear-b-60-years-on) credits Alice Kober's earlier analysis and identifies the Pylos tablet Ta 641. The present lesson's emphasis on independent application of proposed values and the need to acknowledge prior work is appropriate. Exact Bennett/PY Ta 641 claim-level apparatus still needs a specialist check from a fully accessible direct publication; the linked Cambridge `process.pdf` has **not** been independently read here, so this is not a claim to verify every technical step.

### C2-07 — Lefkandi primary site nuance and image provenance (low)

#11 correctly presents the monumental Toumba building, **two human burials and four horses**, dating around **950 BCE**. The [British School at Athens project account by excavation director Irene Lemos](https://www.bsa.ac.uk/2019/01/24/lefkandi-textile-on-display-in-the-national-archaeological-museum/) supports this. A brief [BSA digital fieldwork listing](https://digital.bsa.ac.uk/fieldwork.php?project=873) itself summarises “two horses” instead; the more detailed director's account supports four. Record the difference in external summarization and use the excavated report/detailed interpretation for any further precision. Do not silently “fix” the lesson to two from a compressed catalog summary.

### C2-08 — Early Bronze Age Dhaskalio manufacturing (spot-check passed in scope)

The [Cambridge Keros-Naxos fieldwork report](https://www.arch.cam.ac.uk/research/projects/current-projects/keros-project/2015-2018-keros-naxos-seaways) explicitly discusses Dhaskalio construction, craft specialization and excavated metallurgical workshop; #6 uses this as a differentiated evidence case, not as proof that all Aegean workshops were administered by one authority.

## Chapter-to-chapter recommendation

- At the start of #6, refer back only briefly to the geography lesson's **constraint-versus-choice** idea; focus on *what was made, how labor was organized and how power varied locally*.
- #7 and #8 should contrast **different types** of palace evidence rather than imply an uninterrupted, uniform transition from the “Minoan” to a single Mycenaean state.
- #9 should keep Alice Kober and Bennett prominent and center the testable *process*; #8 already teaches what the tablets reveal *about administration*.
- #10 should teach **how historians test competing explanations** and separate destruction date from longer transformation.
- #11 should move rapidly from the postpalatial recap into **Lefkandi, pottery, selective burial evidence and new contexts of writing**, not give another long “Dark Ages were not dark” lesson.
