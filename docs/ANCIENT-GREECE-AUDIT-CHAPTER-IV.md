### C4-02 — Hard-label inflation and extremely implausible wrong choices (**medium-high**)

All **42 of 42** questions across #19–24 are tagged `hard`; actual rich matching/ordering tasks may be intellectually more substantial than their old numeric fallback and must be separately exercised. Conventional/spot/blank examples currently too easy to eliminate:

- #19 `persian-ionia-agency`: wrong answers assert Miletus ruled the entire Persian Empire or all Greek-speaking cities always made identical choices.
- #20 `empire-foundation-choices`: one nuanced change-over-time explanation vs an alleged initial consensual empire plan or “Athens never led.”
- #21 `war-404-not-end`: “Athens ceased to be an inhabited city” and “404 was the full democratic restoration” are implausible alongside the careful correct option.
- #22 `crisis-mantinea-boundary`: three final/victory/inevitability absolutes make the cautious interpretation obvious.
- #23 `philip-terrain-royal-power`: mineral resources supposedly produce unlimited royal income and uniform obedience.
- #24 `alexander-323-open-succession`: the only impossible claim is that all successor states were agreed permanently at Alexander's death, though the same fact was already taught within the lesson.

A demanding assessment needs **two genuinely plausible competing interpretations** bounded by source evidence, not the only answer without sweeping “all, none, always.” The course may intentionally include some easy retrieval; mark these accurately instead of rewriting every item. Do not change scored question meaning/versions without frozen-history isolation tests.

### C4-03 — Secondary repeated methods vs real historical insight (**medium**)

The useful historical-literacy refrain “an inscription/ancient author/painting does not directly reveal all people” is prominent in #19 (Behistun/Persian tablets vs Herodotus), #20 (tribute quota ≠ all public spending), #21 (Melian Dialogue not verbatim), #22 (promised autonomy vs actions), #23 (political speeches vs neutral reports), and #24 (contemporary diary vs later narrative). This is **purposeful applied evidence criticism** but can sound monotonous unless every instance names a **different inferential error** and empowers a fresh decision. Audit adjacent paragraphs during copyediting and delete the generic caveat only where it is truly redundant, leaving the concrete case.

### C4-04 — Cross-lesson chronology/agency (**medium**)

- #19's Persia is a complex empire with its own records, not the empty opposite of Greek unity; Herodotus depicts different alliances, as #2 already showed.
- #20's Delian alliance is not automatically Athens' pre-agreed empire; post-479 continuing Persian power is real but cannot justify every coercive measure.
- #21 421 “Peace” does not equal the end of all campaigns and allies' separate priorities. A 430 BCE epidemic's precise pathogen is **not established** by present evidence.
- #22 `mē mnēsikakein` in 403 describes constraints/selected exceptions for prosecution, not literal erasure of memory, mass enfranchisement, or identical reconciliation for everyone.
- #23 the aftermath of Chaeronea and Corinthian League oath illustrates a declared common peace **with asymmetrical enforcement**; do not call this a complete unitary Greek state.
- #24 331 contemporaneous Babylonian diary and 323 death diary cannot establish the complete battle narrative, poisoning theory, or a settled successor-state map. Never treat late imperial narratives as eyewitness transcripts.

These key distinctions are already reflected in the scripts examined. A full scholarly source-by-source pass is still required.

### C4-05 — Selected independent source cross-checks (bounded, not full academic sign-off)

- [Athenian tribute quota list, 454/3 BCE, IG I³ 259](https://www.atticinscriptions.com/inscription/IGI3/259) directly specifies **1/60** of tribute dedicated to Athena, affirming #20's distinction between tribute assessment and this inscribed share; fragmentary inscription **does not** give comprehensive expenses.
- [Second Athenian Confederacy decree, RO 22 / IG II² 43](https://www.atticinscriptions.com/inscription/RO/22) records advertised autonomy and league terms dated **378/7 BCE**; its promises cannot independently certify subsequent political conduct in every member city.
- [Babylonian astronomical diary, Gaugamela 331 BCE](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/oriental-varia/a-contemporary-account-of-the-battle-of-gaugamela/) gives a contemporary non-Greek narrative witness, while the [323 BCE death diary](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/oriental-varia/a-contemporary-account-of-the-death-of-alexander/) records Alexander's death, not a clinical diagnosis. Read these alongside the late Greek accounts rather than treating either source as a complete historical record.
- [Tripod of Plataea, Greek Ministry of Culture/Delphi](https://delphi.culture.gr/the-tripod-of-plataea/) is a valuable object-level context for #19's changing commemorative text; interpretation of the exact recutting sequence deserves dedicated epigraphic specialist review.
- **Source access boundary:** some Oxford/Cambridge studies in lesson bibliographies were available as abstracts/metadata; they were not claimed read in full. All checked passages are limited to the precise claim named above.
