## 1. Questions, learning objectives and historical decision

**Central historical puzzle:** A corroded bronze object from a shipwreck, recognized as geared in 1902, preserves gear teeth and incomplete inscriptions. What can these material traces and modern scans establish about the astronomical machine—and which complete-looking displays exist only as models?

After reading, an adult should (1) distinguish shipwreck chronology from manufacture and ownership; (2) understand why gear ratios represent cycles rather than live celestial sensing; (3) explain the functions of the Metonic, Saros and Olympiad dials without assuming global eclipse visibility; (4) distinguish a surviving mechanical feature, an interpreted inscription and a reconstructed missing planetary drive; (5) appreciate technological labour and transregional intellectual exchange without inventing a specific maker or universal diffusion; and (6) identify modern replicas and limitations of reconstruction.

**No overlap with #27:** the lesson is not a general history of astronomy, Euclid or scientific methods. **No overlap with #29:** not a re-telling of Hellenistic courts, diplomacy or state formation. The bridge between #29 and #30 is specifically the social/technical conditions behind one surviving object. The conclusion is an evidence-led capstone for the path rather than a technological-progress myth.

## 5. Uncertainties and interpretations still requiring specialist QA

1. Manufacturing date: ranges and inferred eclipse dial epochs cannot be conflated; shipwreck context gives **terminus ante quem** only.
2. The specific manual input and full original housing are not fully preserved; use qualified phrasing.
3. Lunar anomaly gearing and number of teeth in missing trains involve interpretation.
4. Back dials have substantial archaeological support; planetary front model remains **underdetermined by missing physical parts**. The 2021 article expressly does not claim a replica.
5. Inscriptions vary in legibility and restoration; **check the 2026 NYU-record corrigendum** before expert approval.
6. The calendar month names and games are not a maker's signature or guaranteed place of manufacture.
7. A SAROS mark is not a prediction of eclipse visibility in every locality.
8. Commons descriptions of older museum photos contain **outdated speculative claims** about navigation, Posidonius, Rhodes and specific manufacturing year. The lesson expressly avoids treating these captions as primary scholarly evidence.
9. The 2005 Price reconstruction is historical museum reconstruction, not full surviving instrument; the modern artistic synthesis cannot supply missing physical evidence.
10. Ancient literary mentions of geared astronomy are context, not proof that this particular device belonged to any named author.
