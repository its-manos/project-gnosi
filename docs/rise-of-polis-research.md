# Gnosi — Ancient Greece 12 / `rise-of-polis`

2026-11-01 · **Integrated, NOT Validated or Approved**

## Editorial decision and title
- Original working title: «Η γέννηση της πόλης-κράτους».
- Final visible title: **«Πόλις: άνθρωποι, γη και κοινοί κανόνες»**.
- Rationale: the original suggests a single birth; the new title foregrounds the separable human, territorial and institutional dimensions, without assuming democracy or a uniform urban form.
- Objective: Explain how Greek civic communities acquired shared practices, rules, responsibilities and membership during an uneven Early Iron Age/Archaic development; evaluate what archaeology, contemporary inscriptions and later literature can each prove.
- Narrative: start from the Dreros magistracy restriction as a rare direct early rule, distinguish polis/asty/chora/politai, move to Zagora for collective work without documented polity, examine elites and ancient textual testimony, end with regional variation and alternatives to canonical city-state formation.
- Reading estimate: 12 minutes for 23 Greek paragraphs and five checkpoints, editorial estimate only; not native-timed. No word/paragraph quota was imposed.

## Claim-to-source research ledger
1. **Definition**: a polis can denote built city, territory, citizens, and institutions rather than identical physical/social sets. Mogens Herman Hansen, *Polis: An Introduction to the Ancient Greek City-State* (Oxford University Press, 2006), chs. 7, 10, 17. Book DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199208494.001.0001 ; chapter abstracts https://academic.oup.com/book/47289/chapter-abstract/422665221 , https://academic.oup.com/book/47289/chapter-abstract/422665152 . Caution: abstract and excerpts, not complete text, inspected.
2. **Varied emergence without singular founding date**: Hansen (2006), chapter 7 https://academic.oup.com/book/47289/chapter-abstract/422665102 ; John Ma, *Polis: A New History of the Ancient Greek City-State from the Early Iron Age to the End of Antiquity* (Princeton University Press, 2024), https://doi.org/10.2307/jj.9446862 . Their precise models need not share the same threshold for 'emergence'; avoid hard calendar year of origin.
3. **Dreros law**, mid-/late seventh century BCE: Giacinto Falco (2024), *Gagarin – Perlman Dr1 (Nomima I, 81). Law of Dreros on the prohibition of holding the office of kosmos more than once within ten years (mid-7th century BCE)*, University of Milan Politeia, https://politeia.unimi.it/en/2024/02/09/gagarin-perlman-dr1-nomima-i-81-legge-di-dreros-sul-divieto-di-reiterazione-della-carica-di-kosmos-prima-di-dieci-anni-meta-vii-secolo-a-c-ca/ ; Maria Ilaria De Rossi (2017), *Dreros Law*, peer-reviewed Axon (Ca' Foscari), https://doi.org/10.14277/2532-6848/Axon-1-2-17-01 . Competing readings exist for the precise meaning of sanctions and whether certain named groups were officials. No assertion of universal voting, stable democratic council, or precise election mechanism.
4. **What an early inscription proves**: *The Athenian Empire and epigraphic cultures*, Journal of Hellenic Studies (Cambridge Core, 2024), https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-hellenic-studies/article/athenian-empire-and-epigraphic-cultures/6D6E6C73CA7FAF1F8ED8A5C176843984 ; distinguish survival of public writing from invention of customary rules.
5. **Zagora settlement and chronology**: Zagora Archaeological Project, *About Zagora*, https://zagoraarchaeologicalproject.org/the-site/about-zagora/ ; Greek Ministry of Culture / Ephorate of Antiquities of the Cyclades, *A travelogue of the archaeological sites on Andros*, https://cyclades.culture.gov.gr/en/location/a-travelogue-of-the-archaeological-sites-on-andros/ ; Australian Archaeological Institute at Athens, *Past Projects*, https://aaia.gr/past-projects/ .
