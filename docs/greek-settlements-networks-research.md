# Gnosi — Ancient Greece 13 / `greek-settlements-networks`
2026-11-01 · **Integrated, NOT Validated or Approved** · Chapter III «Ο κόσμος των πόλεων»

## Editorial decision
- **Working title:** «Από τη Μασσαλία ως τη Μαύρη Θάλασσα».
- **Final title:** «Νέοι οικισμοί, παλιοί γείτονες: πέρα από το Αιγαίο».
- **Why:** The itinerary-style working title suggests an inventory of sites and treats existing inhabitants as invisible. The final title foregrounds unequal encounters, land and shared spaces while recognizing diverse settlement models. It does not imply an organized campaign by one Greek state.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish a transported object, a contact port, enduring occupation and retrospective foundation narrative; explain regional variety and the agency of communities already inhabiting the coasts.
- **Narrative:** Four deliberately contrasting cases: (1) Pithekoussai/Ischia for material culture, craft, diverse migrants and mobility; (2) Syracuse/Ortygia for land, indigenous people and the later account of expulsion; (3) Massalia for Phocaean exchange and changing foundation memory; (4) Berezan/north Black Sea for the archaeological sequence from imports to settlement to a written letter. A final vocabulary section evaluates `ἀποικία`, `ἐμπόριον`, `μητρόπολις`, `οἰκιστής` and modern 'colonization'.
- **Extent:** 25 new stable Greek paragraphs in five sections, **1,413 prose words**, plus intro, sources and five reasoning questions. **12-minute approximate reading** including occasional reflection; not stopwatch-tested. Varied structure: evidence-led case comparison, not the polis-institution sequence of #12.
- **Geographical scope:** Bay of Naples (Ischia), southeastern Sicily (Syracuse), Rhône littoral (Marseille), northern Black Sea at Dnieper–Southern Bug estuary (Berezan, Ukraine). There is **no single implied chronological route** between these sites.

## Chronology and competing interpretations
- Earliest case studied: Pithekoussai, mid-eighth century BCE; Syracuse traditionally c. 734/733 BCE; Berezan evidence develops late seventh/early sixth; Massalia c. 600 BCE. **No sequential migration path is asserted.**
- “Greek-style” manufactured goods, artistic style, graves, building plans, residence, language and political self-definition have different evidentiary thresholds.
- Pithekoussai's status as emporion, settlement or polis is not settled by one binary label; early residence is stronger than identifying all residents as Euboean.
- The details of Syracuse's eighth-century conflict cannot be fully reconstructed from Thucydides alone.
- The Massalia marriage story may have legitimated relationships but has **no contemporary corroboration** for specific ceremony/names.
- The Black Sea's early Greek imports and permanent resident contexts do not support universal dates or direct Milesian governmental command.
- Indigenous communities were agents of exchange, control of land and sometimes conflict. Missing evidence is not proof of an absence of people.
- Do not map modern/early modern empire models onto all early Archaic sites; do not erase recorded expulsions or inequality.
