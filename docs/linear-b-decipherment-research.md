**Date:** 2026-11-01 · **Stable ID:** `linear-b-decipherment` · **Original title:** «Η γραφή που έκρυβε ελληνικά» · **Status:** Integrated only (not Validated or Approved).

## Purpose, editorial architecture and reading time
Answer how researchers established that Linear B encodes Mycenaean Greek, why the result is testable rather than a lucky guess, whose scholarship was indispensable, and what decoding does not tell us. Five narrative movements: (1) find contexts and the nature of the script; (2) Bennett and Kober’s constraints; (3) Ventris’s proposals and Chadwick’s philological collaboration; (4) Blegen’s independent PY Ta 641 test; (5) decipherment, translation and continuity limits. Distinct from the Pylos administrative tour in lesson 8. Original introduction begins with lost reading conventions rather than a rhetorical question or simulated archaeological scene.
- **21 stable paragraphs** (4 + 4 + 5 + 4 + 4); **five anchored version-1 questions** at positions **4, 8, 13, 17, 21**. Estimate **12 minutes** including questions; check reading time after device preview. Visible title names Linear B and its evidence rather than suggesting a magical decipherment. No arbitrary section length rule.
- **Scope of uncertainty:** Knossos first tablet from Kalokairinos is probable, not certain; archaeological find years are distinguished from tablet composition; sign class and syllabic model are secure, individual sign readings and broken words sometimes disputed. Early Greek is not proof of a single Mycenaean state, universal literacy, an unbroken literary text tradition or a direct Linear B → Greek alphabet evolution. Linear A remains undeciphered.
- Cross-lesson coverage map: `mycenaean-palaces` = Pylos stores, positions and economy; **this lesson** = decipherment method and history; `minoan-crete` = Cretan settlements and modern restorations; `aegean-bronze-age` = comparative household/material culture; `palace-collapse` = palace destruction and its aftermath; `geometric-greece` = Early Iron Age communities and alphabetic writing; `greek-history-through-evidence` and `archaeology-methods` = general source/archaeological methods.
