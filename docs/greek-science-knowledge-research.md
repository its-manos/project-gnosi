# Lesson 27 — «Πώς ήξεραν ότι είχαν δίκιο;» — research, sources, media and QA
_2026-11-02 · `greek-science-knowledge` · Ancient Greece 27/30 · Chapter V · **Integrated, NOT Validated or Approved**._

## 1. Learning objective and editorial decision
Readers should distinguish a reported phenomenon, a proposed cause, mathematical proof, measurement and predictive modelling; see how an investigation could improve arguments yet preserve errors; and recognise the Greek-speaking eastern Mediterranean as one participant in broader learned and practical networks. **This is not a claim that ancient people used today's category or experimental protocols of “science.”**

Final title **«Πώς ήξεραν ότι είχαν δίκιο;»** replaces working title «Οι αρχαίοι ερευνητές και τα όρια της γνώσης». The question is more focused and reflects the actual story.

**Narrative:** four movements, 24 stable paragraphs:
1. `science-01–06` — A seizure and the so-called sacred disease; critique of causes; clinical observations vs phlegm theory; cult and practical medicine coexist; the diverse, multiply authored Hippocratic Corpus.
2. `science-07–12` — Drawing vs demonstrating; Euclid's Elements at Hellenistic Alexandria; the much later Oxyrhynchus papyrus; practical surveying, workmanship and less-documented contributors.
3. `science-13–18` — Babylonian diaries; careful records combined with omens; transfer of parameters into Greek-language astronomy; retrograde motion, modelling and Ptolemy (explicitly Roman imperial).
4. `science-19–24` — Eratosthenes' Earth-size estimate and the uncertainty of surviving accounts; different standards for different inquiries; `technē` and `epistēmē` as shifting historical vocabulary; selective survival; return to the patient.

**Reading time:** 1,195 whitespace-delimited Greek narrative words (intro + 24 paragraph texts; JSON structural metadata, quiz/options, credits and sources excluded), at 160 words/minute = 7.47 minutes. Six interpretation checks budgeted separately at ~1.5 minutes, rounded up to **9 minutes** (`readingMinutes: 9`). Readers who inspect illustrations or take longer on feedback may need more time. Automated native reading-time measurement not available.

## 3. Evidence ledger, interpretations and disputes

| Claim / example | Evidence available | What remains uncertain / correction to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The «sacred disease» author criticises miracle cures and offers a physical account | *On the Sacred Disease* excerpts; Walshe Oxford abstract; van der Eijk Cambridge summary | Do **not** claim he rejected the gods or that his explanation of epilepsy (phlegm, airflow) is medically correct. Author/date attribution approximate; not securely a personal Hippocrates work. |
| Symptom observation need not validate physiological cause | The treatise's sections on symptoms and bodily fluids; current clinical understanding used only to mark theory incorrect | Avoid retroactive diagnosis of an individual ancient patient; no invented patient case history. Opening is a hypothetical vignette. |
| Hippocratic Corpus is diverse | Cambridge Ancient History medicine chapter abstract (anonymous varied authors) | No secure attribution of every treatise to Hippocrates; avoid a single coherent «school» methodology. |
| Religious and practical healing could coexist | van der Eijk summary and broader ancient sanctuary context | No simplistic «doctors rejected temples» narrative; no claim that a particular patient attended both. |
| Euclidean proof reasons from assumptions; Elements draws on earlier mathematics | Clay edition, Jones 2005 excerpts, UT Austin Euclid introduction | Euclid's biography is extremely poorly attested. No individual discovery priority for every theorem; figure does not itself constitute proof. |
| Oxyrhynchus geometry papyrus | UPenn OPenn E02748 catalogue and Commons file page; MAA independent entry | The **physical surviving copy** is Roman-period, centuries after ca. 300 BCE. OPenn dates 3rd–4th c CE, MAA puts it near the late 1st c CE. Flag divergent dating and investigate at editorial approval. File metadata separately has conflicting licence labels (see below). |
| Measurement and applied geometry | Lewis 2001 publisher summary; Oxford geometry papyri excerpt | General surveying/trades supported; particular individuals and exact instruments in specific centuries not documented. Evidence for ancient early field techniques uneven. |
| Akkadian astronomical diaries | Stevens 2024 OCD abstract; BM catalogue; selected Commons tablet description | Dates span many centuries; **not Greek-language archives** and do not prove a direct route for each table. Diaries include non-astronomical events and interpretations. |
| Transfer of mathematical astronomy | Steele 2011 abstract; BM tablet 55555 catalog description; Library of Congress | Do not assume all Greek astronomy directly copied tablets or that transfers were unidirectional; Egyptian and other contributions are underrepresented by this short narrative. |
| Ptolemaic models predict while geocentric ontology is wrong | Library of Congress astronomy essay; Carman & Díez 2015 abstract; Okruhlik publisher extract | Claudius Ptolemy belongs to **2nd c CE Roman Alexandria**, not Classical Athens. Avoid literal descriptions of physical planetary orbits from purely mathematical fits. |
| Eratosthenes' Earth-size determination | Carman & Evans 2015 Isis abstract; Goldstein 1984 abstract | Later Cleomedes paraphrase; 250,000 vs 252,000 stades; direct observational precision disputed. No confidently reconstructed modern classroom experiment presented as his actual working notes. |
| Meaning of Greek epistemic vocabulary | Contextual historical use in medical, mathematical and philosophical literature | **`technē`** and **`epistēmē`** have no one universal ancient definition; short illustration is not a philological dictionary or a timeless division between technicians and theorists. |
| Whose names survive | Existing studies of manuscript transmission; material artefacts and trade practices | Many scholars, copyists, practitioners, assistants and labourers remain anonymous. No unsupported assignment of specific discovery to unnamed groups. |

**Opposing interpretations that remain explicitly visible:** How far *On the Sacred Disease*'s religious argument goes (van der Eijk vs modern «secularisation» shorthand); the composition and editorial history of *Elements*; Penn E02748 dating and image-licence metadata; whether Eratosthenes' procedure was precise shadow measurement or a constructed estimate; how predictive success bears on the physical reality of Ptolemy's geocentric system. A historical error is not always an immediately recognised error: later scholarship has retrospective knowledge unavailable to contemporary readers.
