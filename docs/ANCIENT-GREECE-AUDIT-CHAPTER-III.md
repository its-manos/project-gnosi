## Findings for follow-up

### C3-01 — Revisited identity and citizenship concepts (medium)

The shared/non-shared Greek civic identity first established in **#2** returns in **#12** (polis), **#13** (settlements) and **#18** (sanctuaries). This is conceptually valid progression but can become repetitive if each repeats “one culture, not one state” as an independent revelation. Use concise explicit references to #2 and advance into new evidence:
- #12 should use **office-holding rules and civic space**; not replay Greek identity.
- #13 should foreground **local inhabitants, mobility and contested founding narratives**; not repeat the geography lesson's port network.
- #18 should focus on **specific institutions and unequal participation** at shared cult sites; not present the absence of a single government as the central new discovery.

Likewise #14 (Sparta), #15 (citizenship) and #17 (hoplites) repeatedly caution that military or social role does not equal equal voting rights; each should apply a new case rather than restate the premise.

### C3-03 — Source methods and selective survival must not turn into filler (medium)

The content responsibly distinguishes archaeological evidence, contemporary writing, later retelling and reconstruction. Yet phrases such as “a photo does not reveal all people,” “a literary scene is not minutes,” and “a single find does not prove an entire government” recur. Keep these where they support **new specific inference**—Dreros legal attribution; Massalia founding story; Spartan late texts; fourth-century court oratory; Chigi iconography; oracles transmitted by Herodotus. Remove generic duplicates only after comparing actual adjacent paragraphs and learning checkpoints.

### C3-04 — Dreros `kosmos` ten-year rule (bounded external corroboration)

The [University of Milan Politeia full-text inscription review](https://politeia.unimi.it/en/2024/02/09/gagarin-perlman-dr1-nomima-i-81-legge-di-dreros-sul-divieto-di-reiterazione-della-carica-di-kosmos-prima-di-dieci-anni-meta-vii-secolo-a-c-ca/) supports the mid-seventh-century BCE restriction and explicitly discusses disputed interpretation of sanctions and civic body. The lesson's careful language is appropriate; it does not infer universal voting rights at Dreros.

### C3-05 — Athenian citizenship stages (bounded external corroboration)

The [Cambridge chapter summary on deme membership and the 451/0 BCE dual-parent requirement](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/democracy-and-participation-in-athens/privileges-and-the-opportunities-of-the-citizen/14CA33DF157137C6845754E4555F8FA1) matches #15's distinct temporal stages. Maintain a conceptual distinction between **male political franchise**, **family/civic identity** and **other religious/social rights**: a woman may be classed as an Athenian in some contexts without voting in the assembly. #16's separate discussion of a foreign merchant's standing in a specific maritime suit should not imply the same political citizenship.

### C3-06 — Hoplite equipment does NOT prove universal democratic transformation (bounded corroboration)

[Peter Krentz, *Warfare and Hoplites*, Cambridge Companion to Archaic Greece (public summary)](https://doi.org/10.1017/CCOL9780521822008.004), explicitly states that the once-standard “hoplite revolution” narrative has been challenged across its component assumptions. #17's separation of arms, formation, sources and political change is justified. **Access:** public publisher summary, not complete paid chapter. No claim to verify every technical description of the Chigi vase or shield fabrication.

### C3-07 — Sanctuaries and Kyniska (bounded corroboration)

The [International Olympic Committee's historical account](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/ancient-olympic-games) notes that **Kyniska** won as an owner of chariots/horses, **not** as a woman driving in a men's event, in 396/392 BCE. #18's distinction between **ownership-based victory** and general female inclusion is important. The festival truce should not be described as universal suspension of all Greek war; the content appropriately distinguishes protected festival travel from general peace. Specialist primary-source review of individual truce episodes remains for historian sign-off.
