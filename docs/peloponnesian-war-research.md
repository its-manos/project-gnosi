## Geography and chronology
- Attica/Piraeus: walls kept maritime supply open but exposed rural households to displacement; no direct pathogen diagnosis.
- Corcyra and Corinth: strategically independent sea powers amid 433 disputes; distinguish Epidamnus and Potidaea; avoid modern nation-state language.
- Southwestern Peloponnese (Pylos/Sphacteria): terrain-based raid and Spartan captives in 425; modern photo shows present landscape only.
- Northern Aegean (Amphipolis): changing allied loyalties and resources; Thucydides' personal exile context.
- Melos: 416, after 421 treaty but before 415 Sicily; dialogue is composed.
- Syracuse/Ortygia: Sicily 415–413, local defenders act; photographed architecture contains later building phases.
- Decelea and Hellespont: later land and naval pressures; Persian regional actors and financial diplomacy.
- 404: Athens surrenders; stop before Thirty Tyrants, restoration, Spartan/Theban hegemony and King's Peace (Lesson 22).
