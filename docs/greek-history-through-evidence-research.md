## Editorial thesis and boundaries
History is not a direct recording of events. It is a reasoned reconstruction from unevenly preserved traces: contemporary material witnesses, official inscriptions, ancient histories, later biographical writing, and modern interpretations. Each kind of source supports some conclusions and leaves others uncertain. This essay uses a sequence of evidence types rather than lesson 1's House Θ archaeological narrative or lesson 2's geographical/political identity comparison. It does not reuse Herodotus 8.144, teach archaeological excavation techniques, retell the Mycenaean era, or revisit the established citizenship lessons.

**Narrative structure:** surviving traces (3 paragraphs) → ostrakon and official decree (4) → Thucydides and near-contemporary narrative (4) → later biography and modern interpretation (3) → Plangon's missing personal voice and warranted uncertainty (4). **18 unique anchored paragraphs / 5 sections / 3 contextual questions / reading estimate 7 minutes**. Unlike a fictional mystery, no reconstructed dialogue or emotional inner world is invented.

**Evidence controls:** The essay does not equate *primary* with *true*, *later* with *false*, or *silence* with absence of agency. It separates directly visible facts from interpretations and unknowns. Avoid overextending the dated Themistocles ostrakon to a specific historical outcome; distinguish Plangon's image as a modern photograph of an ancient funerary stele. The photographed decree (IG I³ 156) was recorded by the source as a boule decree and should not be generalized as the universal format of decrees.
