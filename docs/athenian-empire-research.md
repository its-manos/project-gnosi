## Objective and editorial judgment

Explain the contingency of the post-479 BCE maritime alliance and the uneven transformation from common protection against Persian power to Athenian-controlled collections, fleet deployment, sanction and intervention. Do not pretend that an Athenian empire was legally born in a single meeting on Delos. The final title is **«Από τη Δήλο στην αθηναϊκή ηγεμονία»**: it names the movement of institutional power, is shorter and less mechanical than the working question, and avoids declaring the early alliance an empire.

**Narrative concept:** begin with the inscribed financial monument rather than a fictional founding scene; then explain a postwar problem → original coalition and its headquarters → the ship-versus-money asymmetry → Naxos/Thasos and the meaning of compelled membership → treasury and fragmentary quota lists → Erythrai/Chalkis local decrees → Persians, finance, plural allied responses, and the unpredictable future. 6 sections, **26 stable `empire-01`–`empire-26` paragraphs**, approximately **1,603 Greek space-separated words** excluding intro and exercises, **15-minute estimated reading including six questions**. The last paragraph deliberately stops before the detailed Peloponnesian War of Lesson 21.
