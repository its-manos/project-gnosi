## Narrative logic and coverage / overlap
1. **336–335:** succession, court violence, League renewal, northern security, Thebes/Athens and the human cost of Thebes.
2. **334 context:** Persian satrapies/royal networks, Darius III, overlapping interests, propaganda of liberation, resources, transport and fleets.
3. **334–332/331:** Granicus, choices of western Anatolian cities, Issus, Phoenician coastal control, Tyre/Gaza, Egypt and Alexandria.
4. **331–330:** Gaugamela, Babylon and Susa, Persepolis fire and contested motives, Darius's death, distinction between defeating an army and exercising rule.
5. **Babylonia/administration:** local priests and satrapal continuity, diplomatic adaptation, court ritual, Iranian elites and Macedonian dissent, mainland Greece's continuing agency.
6. **329–327:** resistance in Sogdiana/Bactria, local alliances, Roxana and court violence.
7. **327–324:** Punjab, Hydaspes, Hyphasis refusal, Indus and Gedrosia costs, Susa marriages and Opis tensions.
8. **323/source method:** Babylon death and uncertain succession; cuneiform records vs late authors Arrian, Diodorus, Curtius, Plutarch and Justin.
- Earlier #19 is the 490/480–479 Persian Wars; #22 404–362 poleis; #23 Philip 359–336. No repeat battle itineraries or historical quiz rewrites. #29 alone will cover the successor kingdoms, #25 the civic theatre.
- Debates: campaign goals transformed; Persian state not inert; exact troop/casualty numbers insecure; the burning of Persepolis has competing explanations; 'fusion' is an over-simple label; changing meanings of royal reverence; later poison and psychobiography stories cannot be medically confirmed.

| Succession/Greece and broad military chronology 336–323 | Ian Worthington, 2017; Briant 1985/2018. The details of court accusations and dramatic speeches are later accounts. |

| Local choices in Anatolia, Tyre, Egypt, Babylon, Persis and Asian interior | Briant 1985/2018; Müller 2024; Worthington 2017. Differing communities and imperial resources prevent 'Greek liberation' as unqualified fact. |
| Gaugamela and Darius's flight/withdrawal | Babylonian Astronomical Diary of 331 as translated by Livius; contrast Arrian/Diodorus/Curtius/Plutarch. Tablet fragmentary and the interpretative wording contested. |
