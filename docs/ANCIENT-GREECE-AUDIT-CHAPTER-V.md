## Registered findings

### C5-01 — Hard-label saturation and obvious distractors (medium-high)

All **38/38** scored questions in #25–30 are tagged `hard`, even though many conventional or spot-the-mistake answers distinguish one qualified statement from three patently absolute claims. For example:
- #25 `hellenistic-succession`: disputes “all kingdoms permanently agreed on the day of Alexander's death,” which is already refuted at the end of #24.
- #26 `stage-audience-unknown`: asserts that complete theatre attendance registers prove no woman ever attended any performance; an implausible false statement.
- #27 `images-pigment-method-limit`: local Egyptian blue automatically becomes uniformly blue sculpture; easily rejected once the paragraph is read.
- #28 `phil-academy-place`: compares original Academy's grounds with modern university graduation/diplomas; obvious modern institutional anachronism.
- #29 `science-proof-measure` **is an actual Ordering card**; its legacy four-option data are insufficient to judge the rich task's difficulty. Treat all actual `ordering` and `matching` questions likewise.
- #30 `antikythera-workshop-claims`: correct limited attribution vs all ancient cities owning identical workshops or machines used at every school.

This is an **assessment calibration** recommendation, not a licence to rewrite question text in place: current versions, historical progress, XP, review events and stale-attempt isolation must remain compatible or be explicitly versioned.

### C5-02 — Full-course method theme risks monotony (medium)

#25 royal letter ≠ complete city feelings; #26 tragic text ≠ actual live performance; #27 preserved pigment ≠ full ancient image; #28 character dialogue ≠ Socrates' exact speech; #29 observation ≠ causal truth; #30 reconstruction ≠ surviving gear. These are genuinely **different** epistemic limits and their objects/examples are valuable. But together with #3 and previous chapters they could feel like six consecutive warnings. Integrate **positive inference** after each warning: *what exactly can the learner reconstruct or predict accurately?* Consider one chapter-level synthesis instead of 6 similarly phrased conclusions, without losing the distinct evidentiary steps.

### C5-03 — Required 24→25 chronology bridge (medium)

#24 ends in Babylon in **323 BCE**, before the later dynastic map was settled. #25 starts with third- and second-century diplomatic and tax documents. It correctly introduces a post-conquest **process** rather than instant partition, but the jump across almost a century should be made explicit through a short timeline/bridge if a real user reports confusion. Do not transplant detailed campaign narrative from #24 or reuse #25's first easy misconception as the chapter's main difficult synthesis.

### C5-05 — Historical evidence: Magnesia, Peplos Kore and dated copies (bounded corroboration)

- #25: the [letter of Antiochus III to Magnesia, 208/7 BCE](https://www.attalus.org/docs/rc/s31.html), the [letter of Attalus I, 208/7](https://www.attalus.org/docs/rc/s34.html), and the [Athenian decree about Magnesia in 208/7](https://www.atticinscriptions.com/inscription/IMagnesia/37) are all independently accessible. They support **distinct royal and civic public responses** to the Artemis Leukophryene festival initiative, not guaranteed general independence or actual opinions of all residents.
- #27: the [Acropolis Museum Peplos Kore (Acr. 679) original record](https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/en/node/22292) explicitly states **red is the undercoat beneath final brown hair**, describes pins and decoration, and presents Artemis only as **possible identification**. The lesson does not mistake present red surface for the final visible hair colour.
- #28: [Stanford Encyclopedia, Socrates](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/) is a scholarly entry for the problem of source genres; a dramatic Protagoras conversation must not be treated as stenographic record. Specialist text-by-text philological approval still pending.
- #29: original `On the Sacred Disease` observations do not validate the phlegm explanation of epilepsy. The Euclid papyrus is **a much later copy**, not Euclid's autograph. [University of Pennsylvania OPenn E02748](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0016/html/e2748.html) dates it to **third–fourth century CE** (different catalogue traditions exist); avoid teaching the copy date as the original work date.
- #30: the [Antikythera 2016 inscriptional article bibliographic abstract](https://www.antikythera-mechanism.gr/bibliography/751) and [2021 open mathematical reconstruction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-84310-w) do **not** prove a uniquely established complete planetary face. The [NYU 2016 research collection](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/71597) now lists an **August 2026 correction**; the existing detailed research ledger records that it corrects specific front-inscription printed **formulas**, with published **tables** unchanged. The corrigendum itself was reviewed in the prior research-first integration, **not successfully re-opened in this audit session**. An independent specialist should confirm the exact content before final scholarly approval.

These checks support **specific claims only**; remaining source references and every question explanation need independent comprehensive review before calling lesson “historically verified.” Do not imply complete paywalled publications were read when they were not.

### C5-06 — Specific pre-release image-rights exceptions (**high**)

1. **#27 painted funerary stele, Giovanni Dall'Orto photo**: the [actual Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:7550_-_Piraeus_Arch._Museum%2C_Athens_-_Painted_stele_-_Photo_by_Giovanni_Dall%27Orto%2C_Nov_14_2009.jpg) describes an atypical permission for free commercial reuse *with author credit*; the app metadata correctly avoids falsely assigning a standardized CC licence. Final rights reviewer must preserve the original permission evidence, exact attribution and any venue/source restrictions; it is **not approved from this metadata check alone**.
2. **#29 Euclid papyrus E02748 photo**: the Commons page labels **CC BY-SA 4.0**, but [OPenn underlying image metadata](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0016/html/e2748.html) simultaneously says image **“Attribution ShareAlike License version 4.0”** and inserts **“CC-BY-2.0”** alongside a **BY-SA 2.0 legalcode link**. This inconsistency is a **real rights/provenance blocker** for unqualified commercial approval until authoritative scope/version is clarified or the image is replaced by an independently clear file.
3. **#30 NYU publication content** is separately marked **CC BY-NC 4.0** on the [institutional archive](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/71597). Referencing its findings in original prose is distinct from reusing its restricted figures/pages; **do not insert the publication's graphics into a commercial app without separate permission**. Current app images come from Commons but still need placement-by-placement inspection.

The six lessons have **18 image placements** (6 covers, 12 inline); **none** is considered completely approved or tested on both devices here.
