## Decision, learning aim, overlap
- **Working title:** «Η Σπάρτη πέρα από τον μύθο του πολεμιστή».
- **Final title:** «Σπάρτη: ποιοι στήριζαν την πόλη των «ομοίων»;».
- **Rationale:** The question invites a source-grounded look at the people and work that supported a restricted citizen body, rather than centring the warrior stereotype or promising an unsupported revelation. It connects helot production, perioikic communities, property, women's lives, syssitia and offices.
- **Aim:** Distinguish Spartiate citizens, perioikoi and helots; explain why participation, land, labour, gender, public institutions and contemporary/retrospective evidence must be examined separately.
- **Form:** Six linked social layers: (1) valley, territory and civic labels; (2) helot labour, Messenia, coercion and agency; (3) perioikoi, property and mess contributions; (4) women and age-based education; (5) political offices, Lycurgus and changing source chronology; (6) the Orthia sanctuary and the Spartan mirage.
- **Length:** 26 new stable paragraphs / six sections; approximately **1,482** main-text Greek words (before addition of intros, captions and feedback), reading estimate **14 minutes** with six interactions. Reading time estimated, not user-tested.
- **Scope boundary:** #12 `rise-of-polis` treated polis formation and mixed political communities generally; #13 `greek-settlements-networks` covered contact with non-Greek communities abroad; #14 focuses on Spartan status, labour and governance. #15 `athens-who-voted` alone treats Athenian citizen franchise; #16 Athenian courts; #17 formation/tactics and #18 interstate sanctuaries remain separate. No repetition of Minoan/Mycenaean economic administration or a new global slavery overview.

## Source-to-claim ledger and evidentiary limits
Source pages were inspected as labelled; paywalled volumes were checked via available abstracts/extracts, not represented as read cover-to-cover. Publication dates and direct evidence must not be conflated.
1. **Citizens and categories** — Andrew J. Bayliss, “Sparta’s civic structure,” in *The Spartans: A Very Short Introduction*, Oxford University Press, **26 May 2022**, DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/actrade/9780198787600.003.0002 (publisher abstract inspected). Supports Spartiate / perioikos / helot hierarchy and mixed offices, not precise census ratios.
2. **Spartiate/Lakedaimonian vocabulary** — Graham Shipley, “Lakedaimon,” in M. H. Hansen and T. H. Nielsen, *An Inventory of Archaic and Classical Poleis*, OUP, **2004**, pp. 569–598, DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198140993.003.0044 (abstract). Free Lakedaemonian versus full Spartiate citizenship is not coextensive; neither category includes helots as free citizens.
3. **New identity and chronology scholarship** — Matt Thompson (University of Nottingham), “Spartiates and Lakedaimonians: the language of identity in Archaic and Classical Sparta,” *Journal of Hellenic Studies*, published online **25 September 2026**, Cambridge University Press, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-hellenic-studies/article/spartiates-and-lakedaimonians-the-language-of-identity-in-archaic-and-classical-sparta/41518842B2F4CB532C5887D8F6243BA7 (open-access article, text inspected). Especially attentive to inscription patterns and the changing use of civic labels; cannot supply complete records for all regions.
4. **Perioikoi:** *Oxford Classical Dictionary*, “perioikoi, ‘dwellers round about’,” https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/549687534 (extract inspected). Emphasizes free local poleis, limited foreign policy, warfare, Gytheum port, uneven autonomy; avoid saying every perioikos a craft worker or slave. Comparative geography: Graham Shipley, “The extent of Spartan territory in the late Classical and Hellenistic periods,” *Annual of the British School at Athens*, **2013**, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/annual-of-the-british-school-at-athens/article/abs/extent-of-spartan-territory-in-the-late-classical-and-hellenistic-periods/E0E61E0482C7C1E2BA51693AA51DA2C9 (abstract), confirms changing territory after the fourth century; do not project its map backward.
5. **Syssitia, economic membership** — Stephen Hodkinson, “syssitia,” *Oxford Classical Dictionary*, **7 March 2016**, DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780199381135.013.6193 (extract inspected). Classical common meals, produce contributions and loss of membership for non-contribution; later reform must not be backdated. Aristotle, *Politics* II.9 (fourth century BCE), via MIT classics https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/politics.2.two.html (primary-source translation checked) criticizes wealth concentration, inheritance and female holdings. Do not treat Aristotle's numerical claims as modern statistics or deny his polemical stance.
6. **Helot coercion and violence** — Thucydides, *History of the Peloponnesian War* 4.80.3–4 (late fifth century BCE), Richard Crawley translation (1914), https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0003/tlg001/perseus-eng6/4.80.1-4.80.5 (text inspected). Account of approximately 2,000 selected helots made to disappear; evidence about a reported event, **not proof of a uniform practice from the eighth century onward**. Tyrtaeus fr. 5–7, seventh century BCE poetic material, preserved in later quotations: M. G. L. Cooley, *Sparta*, Cambridge UP (excerpt available in 2026), https://assets.cambridge.org/97810093/82779/excerpt/9781009382779_excerpt.pdf ; passages concern conquered Messenians and burdens; fragments are not a comprehensive sharecropping schedule. Later citation and interpretation debated.
7. **Women's position** — Sarah B. Pomeroy, *Spartan Women*, OUP, **11 July 2002**, DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780195130669.001.0001 (publisher description inspected). Property and inheritance must be differentiated by class and time, not treated as modern franchise.
8. **Education, infant myth and family** — “Boys, Girls, Family, and the State at Sparta,” *Oxford Handbook of Childhood and Education in the Classical World*, **2013**, https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/38682/chapter-abstract/335918651 (abstract inspected). It expressly rejects systematic state killing of infants with disabilities, nuances the family role and the use of later evidence.
9. **Admiration and gendered norms in primary testimony** — Xenophon, *Constitution of the Lacedaemonians* 1–2, 5 (early 4th c BCE), Edgar Cardew Marchant translation (1925), https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0032.tlg010.perseus-eng2:1/ and https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0032.tlg010.perseus-grc2:5/ (primary opening and book 5 inspected). Xenophon explicitly admires Lycurgus and links girls' exercise to childbearing. A prescriptive admiring work, not a neutral census of women or daily life.
10. **Legitimating lawgiver and Great Rhetra** — Massimo Nafissi, “Lycurgus (2), legendary Spartan,” *Oxford Classical Dictionary*, **2016**, revised **30 October 2019**, DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780199381135.013.3819 (abstract inspected). Lycurgus is a constructed legitimating tradition, not securely dated individual legislator. Plutarch, *Life of Lycurgus* 6, first to second century CE, https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0007.tlg004.perseus-eng2:6/ (text inspected). The Rhetra's presentation is **many centuries later**, not a contemporary constitutional inscription.
11. **Archaeological evidence, sanctuary phases** — British School at Athens, “Sparta Begins: 1906–1910 BSA Sparta Excavations in the SPHS Collection,” **24 February 2022**, https://www.bsa.ac.uk/2022/02/24/sparta-begins-1906-1910-bsa-sparta-excavations-in-the-sphs-collection/ (site inventory inspected). Archaic offerings, ivories, masks, lead figurines; Roman amphitheatre and later layers mean the standing site cannot be equated with the original Archaic sanctuary.
12. **Broader interpretive debate** — *Sparta: Beyond the Mirage*, eds. Anton Powell and Stephen Hodkinson, Duckworth/Swansea proceedings **2002**, available chapter abstracts https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1n357hd (overview and summaries, not complete book). Nikos Birgalias, “Helotage and Spartan Social Organization” and Andrey Eremin, “Settlements of Spartan Perioikoi: Poleis or Kōmai?” show contested categories. Beware replacing a martial idealisation with an equally anachronistic picture of total uniform oppression.
