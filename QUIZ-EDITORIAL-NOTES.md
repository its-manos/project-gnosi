The checkpoint order shaped the questions. For example, Pompeii now begins with the forum and city life; food shops are tested only after their section. Athens asks about childhood at the third checkpoint and the Agora at the fourth. The later archaeological synthesis section remains reading after the fourth checkpoint.

The exercises use short situations such as comparing two photos of Mars, explaining a floating pencil in orbit, evaluating a model-generated citation, choosing a useful geographic comparison, and checking an archival silence. They aim to distinguish a supported conclusion from a plausible overreach.

The difficult-history sets focus on institutions, source perspective, corroboration, agency and historical responsibility. They avoid graphic detail and role-playing as perpetrators. Historical denial is not presented as an equally supported alternative to evidence-based accounts.

The revision uses the existing lesson content as its factual boundary and retains its sources. It does not add a fresh source review or a new factual claim to the lesson body. Difficulty is an editorial target and should be calibrated with learner responses after release.
